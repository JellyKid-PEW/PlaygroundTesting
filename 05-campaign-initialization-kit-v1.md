# Sky Islands Campaign Initialization Kit v1.0

## The Aven Corridor

## 1. Purpose

This document initializes a playable campaign using:

* Persistent Narrative RPG Engine v2
* Narrative Interaction Model
* Sky Islands Reference Repository
* Sky Islands Simulation Layer

It provides enough people, places, pressures, routes, and existing activity for play to begin immediately.

It does not define a main quest.

It does not assume the player joins Lina's crew.

It does not assume the player meets Vera.

It does not assume the player works in the sky professions at all.

The player enters an already-functioning regional community.

## 2. Campaign Scope

### Region

**The Aven Corridor**

A loose commercial and travel region centered on:

* Aven
* Pere
* Dema
* the routes and storm structures connecting them

Aven serves as the default campaign hub.

## 3. Canon Foundation

### Aven

**TEXT CANON**

Aven is a substantial inhabited Sky Island.

Known features include:

* red-stone cliffs;
* terraced settlement;
* densely built rooftops;
* folded bridge-spans;
* a large working port;
* lower-market districts;
* vegetation on the upper cliffs.

Its port wraps around the island's lower face in rings of wood and brass.

The lower market lies beneath folded bridge structures and already feels like an established social and commercial district rather than an adventurer marketplace.

### Pere

**TEXT CANON**

Pere is another inhabited island connected to Aven by a registered commercial lane.

The ordinary Pere–Aven crossing takes roughly five hours in ordinary weather.

The route is established enough that Captain Sen has made it dozens of times.

### Dema

**TEXT CANON**

Dema is connected commercially with Aven.

The Grayline has sought specialist coverage for an Aven–Dema movement involving carriers and a livestock tender.

Everything else concerning Dema in this campaign initialization is seed material.

## 4. Campaign Time

### Default Migration Stage

**CAMPAIGN SEED**

**Stage 1 — Early Preparation**

The ten-year migration is not imminent enough to dominate ordinary conversation.

Most people still describe the current year as normal.

But professionals are beginning to notice preparation.

Examples:

* bridge inspections;
* unusual infrastructure work;
* long-duration shipping contracts;
* increased survey activity;
* warehouse reservations;
* experienced crews receiving early inquiries.

There is no public crisis.

Migration is background pressure.

This is deliberate.

The campaign should have time to establish what ordinary Sky Islands life looks like before the migration becomes unavoidable.

## 5. Current Seasonal Conditions

**CAMPAIGN SEED**

The Aven Corridor is experiencing a relatively wet working season.

General characteristics:

* frequent afternoon rain;
* moderate westward pressure;
* several stable commercial lanes;
* intermittent lightning below working altitude;
* unusually heavy cloud accumulation east of Aven;
* normal wildlife activity overall.

Nothing presently warrants regional alarm.

## 7. Aven — Playable District Structure

Most of the following is CAMPAIGN SEED, built around the existing prose.

### Lower Port

Primary working vessel district.

Contains:

* berths;
* cargo cranes;
* repair access;
* warehouses;
* specialist workshops;
* pier administration;
* weather boards;
* passenger staging.

This is where professional crews are most likely to intersect.

### Lower Market

**TEXT-CANON FOUNDATION / CAMPAIGN EXPANSION**

Built beneath the folded bridge structures.

Crowded.

Practical.

Popular with:

* crews coming off shift;
* dock workers;
* small merchants;
* visiting contractors;
* passengers;
* apprentices.

Food businesses and small workshops often occupy older structural spaces.

### Red Steps

**CAMPAIGN SEED**

A steep residential and commercial district ascending from the lower port.

Named for Aven's red stone.

Contains:

* inexpensive rooms;
* boarding houses;
* laundries;
* neighborhood kitchens;
* repair shops;
* family homes.

Many early-career sky workers live here.

### Upper Terraces

**CAMPAIGN SEED**

Greener, quieter residential and agricultural areas.

Known for:

* orchard terraces;
* small farms;
* civic offices;
* older homes;
* some wealthier households.

People working at the port complain about the climb.

People living above complain about port noise.

Both continue using the same markets.

### Bridge Quarter

**CAMPAIGN SEED**

District surrounding the mechanisms and towers supporting Aven's folded bridges.

Increasingly active because of migration-cycle inspections.

Contains:

* engineering offices;
* survey crews;
* structural workshops;
* records offices;
* temporary labor yards.

Not usually exciting.

Currently becoming economically important.

### Outer Berths

**CAMPAIGN SEED**

Less convenient docks used by:

* cheap carriers;
* independent operators;
* damaged craft;
* vessels awaiting long repairs;
* crews unwilling to pay central berth rates.

Good location for low-cost work and questionable bargains.

Not inherently criminal.

## 8. Recurring Businesses

These are CAMPAIGN SEED unless otherwise noted.

### The Blue Bowl

**TEXT-CANON FOUNDATION**

Small repair business specializing in:

* veil-kite frames;
* sensor assemblies;
* storm-sail fittings;
* cable systems.

Known for good work and terrible scheduling.

Owner:

**Ena Vos**

### Green Door Rooms

Boarding house on the Red Steps.

Popular with temporary contract workers.

Cheap enough to matter.

Clean enough not to apologize.

Owner:

**Toma Bel**

### Low Ring Exchange

Broker cooperative near the working docks.

Posts:

* freight work;
* passenger needs;
* temporary crew positions;
* specialist contracts;
* courier requests.

It is not the only broker system on Aven.

### The Long Table

Communal dining hall and cheap evening kitchen.

Used heavily by dockworkers.

People sit wherever space exists.

Excellent environment for rumor propagation.

### West Rail Baths

Public bathhouse used by crews living in cramped quarters.

Less glamorous than the name suggests.

Very popular.

### Aven Relay House Three

Commercial communication office handling regional relay traffic.

Most ordinary users simply submit traffic.

Professionals sometimes maintain accounts for scheduled reports.

## 13. Background Situation 1 — The Tern's Condenser

### Category

Professional / logistical

### Actors

* Captain Osa
* Oren
* Kel
* several freight clients

### Current State

The Tern has a condenser showing intermittent instability.

Oren says it requires proper workshop time.

Captain Osa believes the vessel can complete two more runs safely before scheduled maintenance.

Neither is necessarily irrational.

### Next Development

The next run is scheduled in two days.

### Without Player

Oren refuses to sign off.

Osa finds another mechanic willing to authorize limited operation.

The vessel probably completes the run.

The argument continues.

### Potential Wake

The player might encounter:

* repair work;
* crew recruitment;
* a delayed shipment;
* an argument at Nine Pins.

## 30. Player Entry Philosophy

The player should enter through ordinary life.

Do not begin:

* dangling from a cliff;
* awakening with mysterious powers;
* as the sole survivor of a disaster;
* being summoned by regional leadership;
* accidentally discovering the secret behind migration.

Unless the player specifically asks for such an opening.

## 39. Session One Default Opening

If the player gives no strong opening preference, use:

**New Arrival / Temporary Work**

The player arrives on Aven late afternoon.

Weather:

> Light rain.

Location:

> Lower Port.

Immediate situation:

> Their expected contact has been delayed approximately forty minutes because a berth assignment changed.

Nothing dangerous is happening.

The player possesses:

* their belongings;
* the name of their contact;
* enough information to find the Low Ring Exchange;
* temporary lodging information.

Around them:

* dock workers are finishing one unloading job;
* another vessel is preparing to leave;
* structural inspectors have marked several market supports;
* someone nearby is arguing about damaged equipment;
* the smell of food reaches the port from above.

The player decides what to do with forty unexpected minutes.

## 40. Why This Opening Works

It allows the player to:

* wait;
* explore;
* eat;
* speak to someone;
* investigate the work board;
* find lodging;
* watch the docks;
* contact someone;
* do nothing.

There is no correct action.

The first meaningful relationship or story may arise from whichever direction they choose.

## 41. Alternative Session One — Local

For an Aven-native player:

Begin during an ordinary morning.

Something in their routine has changed slightly.

Examples:

* inspection markings appeared near work;
* their usual coworker is absent;
* rent increased;
* a regular ship is late;
* a friend has an interview;
* a supplier changed terms.

Start with life.

## 42. Alternative Session One — Professional

For an experienced sky worker:

Begin during job preparation.

Not emergency.

The player is reviewing:

* route;
* equipment;
* client terms;
* crew readiness.

Give them enough professional information to make meaningful preparation choices.

Reward those choices later.

## 43. Initial Rumors

At campaign start, the following may circulate.

Not all are true.

> "Choirbacks are climbing early."

Partially based on real reports.

Interpretation likely wrong.

> "Bridge crews found structural damage."

Exaggerated.

They found ordinary wear.

> "Warehouse prices are going up before winter."

Likely true.

Migration speculation is contributing.

> "Grayline is hiring anyone with a heartbeat."

False.

They are hiring several specialists.

> "Vera crossed Dema to Aven in half the normal time."

Possibly distorted runner gossip.

Do not establish exact truth unless needed.

> "Tal's making enough money to buy his own barge now."

Unknown.

Do not solve unless relevant.

## 44. Initial Player-Facing Information

A new arrival should reasonably know:

* Aven is a significant working island;
* the port is active;
* Pere and Dema are connected regional destinations;
* migration exists as a recurring future reality;
* professional sky work is common;
* the storm is dangerous enough to respect.

They should not automatically know:

* every local crew;
* detailed wildlife behavior;
* insider broker relationships;
* current background situations;
* migration planning details.

## 45. Knowledge by Profession

A professional player should begin with more.

Example:

### Navigator

Understands route structures, forecast age, island wakes.

### Obfuscator

Understands attention management and common equipment.

### Pilot

Understands vessel behavior and practical flight constraints.

### Repair Specialist

Recognizes system conditions and common failure patterns.

### Broker

Understands contracts, clients, reputation.

### Local Civilian

Knows neighborhoods, ordinary transport, local institutions.

Do not make every player learn obvious professional basics through exposition.

## 46. Starting NPC Contact

After character creation, assign the player 2–4 existing relationships appropriate to background.

Examples:

* landlord;
* former coworker;
* sibling;
* broker;
* instructor;
* neighbor;
* client;
* old crewmate.

Not every campaign should begin with the protagonist knowing nobody.

## 47. Relationship Seeding

Each starting relationship should contain:

* what they know about one another;
* one ordinary shared history;
* one established behavioral permission;
* one unresolved or simply unfinished element.

Example:

The player's former coworker may:

* trust their technical judgment;
* routinely borrow tools;
* know they hate early shifts;
* still owe them money from dinner.

No dramatic secret required.

## 48. Starting Possessions

Determine based upon profession.

Avoid generic RPG inventories.

A pilot owns different useful things than:

* a cook;
* broker;
* mechanic;
* apprentice;
* courier.

Personal objects may become narratively meaningful later.

Do not designate them as important in advance.

## 49. Campaign Initialization Procedure

When the player says START NEW CAMPAIGN, the Game Master should:

### Step 1

Ask:

* name;
* approximate age;
* what they currently do or why they are on Aven;
* whether they want to define their past now or discover it through play.

Do not ask twenty questions.

### Step 2

Establish:

* several competencies;
* several limitations;
* current housing;
* current money/resources approximately;
* 2–4 starting relationships.

### Step 3

Load:

* current route state;
* background situations;
* NPC schedules;
* local weather;
* migration stage.

### Step 4

Determine which background developments have occurred before the opening scene.

### Step 5

Begin.

## 50. Player Independence Rule

None of the fifteen initial background situations require player intervention.

If the player does nothing:

all fifteen continue.

Some resolve.

Some worsen.

Some become irrelevant.

Some produce new opportunities.

This is mandatory.

## 51. Session Density Rule

Do not present all fifteen situations.

At Session One the player may encounter signs of only:

one to three.

Others remain hidden.

## 52. First Ten Sessions

Do not force campaign scope to expand quickly.

A satisfying first ten sessions may consist largely of:

* work;
* food;
* travel;
* meeting people;
* getting paid;
* making mistakes;
* learning a route;
* finding housing;
* choosing whether to keep a job;
* developing opinions about people;
* discovering the rhythms of Aven.

Larger patterns may remain peripheral.

## 53. Existing Cast Encounter Guidance

Lina's crew should not appear merely because the Game Master recognizes their names.

A natural first encounter might eventually occur because:

* both crews use Nine Pins;
* both bid on related work;
* they are eating at the Blue Bowl;
* a vessel arrives while the player is present;
* they share a client.

Even then:

the encounter need not become important.

## 54. Vera Encounter Guidance

Vera should initially exist more naturally as:

* a runner wake;
* a name on an arrival notice;
* professional gossip;
* someone briefly crossing a dock;
* a courier another NPC hired.

Do not stage her entrance like a celebrity cameo.

If the player eventually matters to her, earn it.

## 55. Migration Visibility at Campaign Start

Migration should currently appear through perhaps:

* bridge inspection;
* storage reservation;
* long-term contracts.

That is enough.

Do not repeatedly remind the player every session.

## 56. Long-Term Campaign Evolution

If play continues for many in-world months:

The simulation may gradually advance migration pressure.

What begins as:

> yellow inspection paint beneath the market

may eventually become:

> commercial lanes reserved months ahead,

then:

> specialist crews receiving retainers,

then:

> livestock movements,

then:

> major route restrictions,

then finally:

> migration itself.

The player should remember when it was merely paint on an iron rib.

## 57. Narrative Memory Seed

At campaign initialization create an empty Narrative Memory Ledger.

Do not preload significance.

Let play create it.

The regional environment already supplies recurring places capable of accumulating memory:

* the player's home;
* worksite;
* Blue Bowl;
* Low Ring Exchange;
* a favored pier;
* a ship;
* a route.

Meaning should arise from use.

## 58. Campaign Identity

Suggested campaign-state header:

> Campaign: Talkar — Sky Islands
> Region: Aven Corridor
> Hub: Aven
> Migration Stage: Early Preparation
> World Condition: Stable
> Player Importance: Local / ordinary at initialization
> Main Quest: None
> Novel Cast: Independent world actors
> Simulation Active: Yes
> Narrative Memory Active: Yes

## 59. Final Initialization Rule

Do not ask:

> Which storyline does the player want?

Ask:

> Who are they, and what are they doing today?

Then simulate the answer.
