# Talkar — Sky Islands Reference Repository v1.0

## Repository Purpose

This repository defines the current playable setting of the Sky Islands region of Talkar for use by the Persistent Narrative RPG Engine.

It is a living reference document.

It is not intended to permanently lock the setting.

It exists so that setting information can be revised, replaced, expanded, or removed without rewriting the RPG Engine or Narrative Interaction Model.

The Game Master must consult the most current version of this repository whenever setting knowledge matters.

## 1. Status Tags

Every significant entry should be understood according to one of the following statuses.

### TEXT CANON

Established directly in current Sky Islands prose.

This is presently the highest-confidence source for how the region actually functions in narrative practice.

Do not contradict it unless a later repository revision explicitly changes it.

### CURRENT CANON

Established through later worldbuilding decisions and intended to govern the setting, but not necessarily yet demonstrated in current prose.

Treat as authoritative unless revised.

### PROVISIONAL

A developed idea that remains useful but has not been locked.

The RPG may use it carefully, but should avoid building major irreversible setting assumptions on it.

### OPEN

Not yet decided.

The RPG may preserve ambiguity or make small local inventions that do not close the question.

### VERSION CONFLICT

Two existing sources establish different versions.

Do not silently choose between them.

Use whichever element can function without resolving the contradiction, or leave the matter undefined until repository revision resolves it.

### LEGACY

Appears in older Talkar development but has not been confirmed for the current Sky Islands project.

Do not automatically import into play.

### REJECTED / SUPERSEDED

Explicitly abandoned, contradicted by later development, or replaced by a newer concept.

Do not reintroduce unless requested.

## 2. Scope Boundary

**CURRENT CANON**

This repository concerns the Sky Islands region of Talkar.

It does not attempt to explain all of Talkar.

Broader Talkar subjects—including ancient divine history, planetary cosmology, other continents, distant civilizations, future eras, or the ultimate metaphysics of the world—are outside the RPG's default knowledge base.

They may exist.

They should not be imported into ordinary Sky Islands play merely because they appear in older Talkar material.

The Sky Islands campaign should be capable of functioning for a very long time without requiring the player to become involved in the greater cosmological history of Talkar.

## 3. Core Regional Identity

### Floating Islands

**TEXT CANON / CURRENT CANON**

Human civilization in this region exists across islands suspended above immense layers of cloud and magical storm.

The islands are not surrounded by clear blue emptiness.

Beyond their cliffs, cloud may occupy nearly every visible distance: lighter working layers near settled heights and increasingly violent storm regions beneath and between them.

Current prose describes the edge of Pere as opening not onto empty sky but onto white, gray, and green-black cloud, with lightning moving inside the deeper layers and shapes sometimes visible within it.

The islands support:

* settlements;
* ports;
* markets;
* workshops;
* agriculture;
* passenger travel;
* freight traffic;
* professional sky crews;
* long-distance trade.

Civilization here is functional.

The Sky Islands are not a collection of desperate survivors barely enduring an apocalypse.

Dangerous skies are an ordinary condition around which people have built professions, infrastructure, procedures, markets, families, and expectations.

## 4. Relationship to Talkar

### Regional Setting

**CURRENT CANON**

The Sky Islands are part of Talkar.

The RPG should treat the region as part of a larger world without requiring the rest of Talkar to be fully defined.

People may know that other lands and cultures exist.

Those lands do not need to become immediately relevant.

### Greater Cosmology

**OPEN FOR RPG PURPOSES**

Do not automatically use:

* imprisoned gods;
* divine relic politics;
* planetary apocalypse;
* Children of Talkar;
* ancient divine conflicts;
* the deeper nature of Talkar itself;

unless they are deliberately added to a future version of this repository.

Older Talkar documents contain such material, but they are not sufficient authority for importing those systems into the current Sky Islands campaign.

## 5. The Storm

### General Nature

**TEXT CANON / CURRENT CANON**

The storm is a pervasive environmental system surrounding and separating the islands.

It includes ordinary atmospheric phenomena and magical phenomena operating together.

Known factors include:

* cloud mass;
* pressure;
* lightning;
* rainfall;
* magical discharge;
* living signatures;
* island wakes;
* mechanical signatures;
* movement through different vertical layers.

A storm region can possess its own changing rhythm.

For example, the North Fold is described through irregular pressure, broad magical discharge, heavy rain formation, and lightning inside the cloud mass.

The storm should never function as generic fantasy fog.

It possesses structure.

Professionals read that structure.

### Storm Bands / Layers

**TEXT CANON**

The sky possesses meaningful vertical layers.

Current prose refers to creatures rising above the seventh layer, vessels descending part of a layer, and different ecological expectations according to altitude.

Exact total number of layers:

**OPEN**

Exact standardized taxonomy:

**OPEN**

The RPG may refer naturally to known layers where required but should not invent a complete global classification system unless added later.

### Clear Pockets and Folds

**TEXT CANON**

Routes may pass through:

* island wakes;
* clear pockets;
* storm folds;
* pressure systems;
* regions with different lightning or cloud behavior.

These are navigational features rather than abstract biomes.

Current Pere–Aven routing uses Pere's wake, a clear pocket at Marker Glass, the North Fold, and Aven's outer drag.

### Storm Volatility

**CURRENT CANON**

Some corridors are comparatively stable.

Others are dangerous.

Conditions change.

Forecasts age.

Local readings matter.

Professional judgment matters.

A route regarded as routine can become dangerous without becoming inexplicably suicidal.

## 6. Island Wakes

### Basic Principle

**TEXT CANON**

Islands affect the surrounding storm environment.

Professionals can recognize and use an island wake.

Pere's wake can be followed as part of a route, and the sounding basin renders its deeper wash as part of the surrounding environmental state.

Exact physical and magical mechanics:

**OPEN**

Do not explain island wakes beyond what characters reasonably understand unless later established.

## 7. Sky Travel

### Importance

**CURRENT CANON**

Sky travel is central to life between islands.

It carries:

* passengers;
* freight;
* agricultural stock;
* industrial materials;
* messages;
* professionals;
* specialized cargo.

Travel takes meaningful time.

Routes matter.

Weather matters.

Preparation matters.

### Registered Lanes

**TEXT CANON**

Formal routes exist.

Pere to Aven is described as a registered lane with a normal crossing time of approximately five hours in ordinary weather. Captain Sen has personally made the crossing thirty-one times.

This establishes that at least some crossings are routine commercial transportation rather than expeditions.

A route being routine does not mean it is harmless.

### Route Forecasting

**TEXT CANON**

Crews use forecasts but also evaluate their age and compare them with current local conditions.

Relevant factors include:

* pressure;
* cloud mass;
* lightning;
* life activity;
* storm movement.

A four-hour-old forecast is still usable but worth checking against local readings.

### Route Boards

**TEXT CANON**

Physical route boards and route cords are used by navigators.

Lio uses a board with colored cords and brass hooks to represent the intended route.

Do not assume all navigation is abstract magical intuition.

## 8. Skyships

### General

**TEXT CANON / CURRENT CANON**

Multiple vessel classes exist.

Current examples include:

* freight barges;
* cutters;
* runners;
* passenger-capable vessels;
* tenders;
* smaller skiffs.

Different vessels are built for different jobs.

### Lift-Hearts

**TEXT CANON**

Lift-hearts are central components of at least major sky vessels.

The barge Morrow contains a lift-heart roughly the size of a market stall, held within an ironwood frame.

It produces a detectable pulse.

That pulse can affect how surrounding wildlife perceives a vessel.

Exact magical engineering of lift-hearts:

**OPEN**

Do not reduce them to simple engines unless later established.

### Storm-Sails

**TEXT CANON**

Large barges may use storm-sails.

The Morrow carries six.

Their exact propulsion mechanics remain:

**OPEN**

### Barge Characteristics

**TEXT CANON**

Barges are substantial working craft designed for distance and cargo rather than agility.

The Morrow is deep-bellied, blunt-ended, and moves with much greater inertia than the smaller Latch.

Barges may carry:

* industrial plate;
* condenser components;
* agricultural stock;
* bulk food;
* passengers.

They can maintain ordinary scheduled commercial routes.

### Cutters

**TEXT CANON**

The Latch is a small specialized working vessel.

It is built for six.

Its interior includes:

* pilot's position;
* navigation station;
* sensor station;

* cable;
* emergency sailcloth.

It is old, heavily repaired, and professionally functional.

## 9. Obfuscation

### Profession

**CURRENT CANON / TEXT CANON IN PRACTICE**

Obfuscation is a specialized form of sky work concerned with managing how vessels and activity are perceived by dangerous storm life.

An obfuscator does not simply make a ship invisible.

The work includes manipulating patterns of:

* heat;
* magical pulse;
* movement;
* apparent vessel identity;
* living or mechanical signatures;
* attention.

### Core Principle

**TEXT CANON**

The environment contains living creatures that interpret signals.

Successful obfuscation therefore often involves giving those creatures a plausible answer rather than simply erasing the real vessel.

This is a key rule.

The crew may create the apparent presence of:

* empty freight skiffs;
* distant vessels;
* smaller organisms;
* contact calls;
* departure calls.

The objective is frequently to redirect or resolve attention rather than force the creature to fail at perception.

### Veil-Kites

**TEXT CANON**

Veil-kites are deployable pieces of obfuscation equipment.

Four kites around the Latch can produce staggered false lift-heart pulses, presenting the pattern of multiple freight skiffs moving apart.

Their exact construction is not fully established.

### False-Hearts

**TEXT CANON**

False-hearts generate or shape false signatures.

They can imitate rhythmic signals and may be used to create artificial contacts.

They have physical limits.

Sustained use can:

* overheat;
* warp cages;
* crack equipment;
* impose meaningful costs.

A false-heart is therefore not an unlimited magical decoy.

### False Freighters / False Vessels

**TEXT CANON**

Obfuscation can create convincing apparent vessels within a creature's perceptual environment.

These are not necessarily visible holograms.

They function through the signatures by which storm life identifies activity.

### Sounding Basin

**TEXT CANON**

The Latch contains a black sounding basin used by the lead to perceive and manipulate the local pattern.

When active, it renders a second informational landscape including:

* pressure;
* heat;
* living pulse;
* mechanical activity;
* island wake;
* false signatures.

The physical ship remains present while the basin reveals this additional perceptual layer.

Do not treat the basin as a perfect radar screen.

Interpretation and skill remain necessary.

### Sounding Frames

**TEXT CANON**

Dara uses a sounding frame containing silver wires and beads affected by environmental conditions.

It is used to read local conditions and living attention.

Exact physics/magic remain open.

## 10. Obfuscation Doctrine

### Attention Rather Than Aggression

**TEXT CANON**

The professional goal is not automatically to attack storm wildlife.

The crew can:

* divert attention;
* maintain a false answer;
* alter a perceived contact;
* allow a creature to disengage naturally.

Current prose specifically avoids burning a decoy in a way that would leave a choirback searching after the apparent contact abruptly vanished.

### Ethical Boundary

**TEXT CANON**

The crew does not casually redirect dangerous wildlife toward uninvolved travelers.

When Vera's fresh runner wake could have been used as an easier decoy, Lina refuses because doing so would effectively hang a choirback on a passing ship.

This establishes a meaningful professional ethic.

It does not imply every obfuscator follows the same standard.

### Specialization

**CURRENT CANON**

Obfuscators may develop reputations and specialties.

Some crews may be:

* safer;
* cheaper;
* more aggressive;
* better with specific corridors;
* better with particular wildlife;
* better protecting large barges;
* willing to take greater losses.

The professional market remembers performance.

## 11. Storm Wildlife

### General

**TEXT CANON / CURRENT CANON**

The storm supports large magical aerial fauna.

They are animals or beings with their own behavior and sensory logic.

They should not be treated simply as monsters waiting for combat.

Professional travel depends partly upon understanding them.

### Choirbacks

**TEXT CANON**

Choirbacks are enormous storm creatures capable of responding to patterned signatures.

Known characteristics include:

* immense scale;
* translucent crown vanes;
* internal blue-green light;
* large eyes;
* bodies capable of displacing substantial cloud;
* wind-root growing along ridged backs;
* communication or attention through rhythmic calls.

They may interpret a damaged vessel component as a contact signal.

More than one choirback may interact with the same signal.

Their behavior is responsive rather than automatically predatory.

The crew resolves one encounter by answering their signals and then providing a gradual departure call rather than simply cutting the decoys.

### Shellbacks

**TEXT CANON**

Shellbacks exist and can detect at least some vessel signatures.

Current prose establishes that they may hear or detect a cracked condenser but do not necessarily care.

Further biology:

**OPEN**

### Needlewings

**TEXT CANON**

Needlewings exist and are sufficiently familiar to professionals that Rin distinguishes their behavior from choirbacks.

Further biology:

**OPEN**

### Soul Beasts

**CURRENT CANON / TERMINOLOGY NOT FULLY RESOLVED**

Broader Sky Islands development has included storm creatures capable of bonding with people.

At least some specialized travelers may work with bonded storm beasts.

The exact relationship between the term soul beast, ordinary storm wildlife, bonded storm beast, and other magical fauna is not yet fully systematized.

Do not invent a universal taxonomy.

## 12. Bonded Storm Beasts

### Existence

**CURRENT CANON**

Bonded storm beasts are allowed within the setting.

A rider may use one for specialized sky travel.

Bonding should represent a meaningful relationship between living beings, not simply ownership of a magical vehicle.

Exact:

* bonding process;
* rarity;
* species;
* legal status;
* magical mechanism;

remain **OPEN**.

## 13. Vera

### Role

**CURRENT CANON / TEXT CANON FOR PRESENCE**

Vera is an independent Sky Runner / Storm Courier.

Current prose establishes that other crews recognize her as a runner and recognize the characteristic narrow wake of her passage.

She is not automatically part of the main obfuscator crew.

### Professional Niche

**CURRENT CANON**

Vera specializes in precision transportation where speed, discretion, responsiveness, or unusual conditions justify paying a specialist.

Possible work includes:

* urgent messages;
* warnings;
* singular valuable cargo;
* living cargo;
* discreet transportation;
* time-sensitive runs.

She is a luxury or specialist service, not the only functioning courier in the world.

Stable alternatives exist and are often slower.

### Reputation

**CURRENT CANON**

Vera is associated with returning with things that should have been difficult to bring through safely.

She is competent and trusted for unusual jobs.

Do not exaggerate this into legendary invincibility.

### Motivation

**CURRENT CANON**

She is not primarily an adrenaline seeker.

She runs because the work has purpose.

Speed is a tool.

Danger is part of the profession.

### Transport

**VERSION CONFLICT / OPEN**

Development has included:

* a specialized slipship;
* a bonded storm beast.

The later preferred direction leaned toward the bonded beast, but the definitive current implementation has not been locked into the repository.

Until resolved:

Do not establish Vera's permanent transport method as universal canon during RPG play unless necessary.

She can be referred to simply as a runner.

## 15. Lead Identity Conflict

**TEXT CANON**

Current written Chapter One explicitly names the new lead Lina.

She replaces a prior lead named Tal.

### Later Development

**VERSION CONFLICT**

Earlier development used names including:

* Nyx;
* Nira;

and later discussion left the lead's final name unresolved.

Therefore:

The repository does not yet declare Lina the immutable final-series name.

For RPG purposes:

If the RPG is explicitly using current Chapter One continuity, use Lina.

Do not reintroduce Nyx or Nira as current canon.

## 16. Tal

**TEXT CANON**

Tal was the crew's previous lead.

He left for substantially better pay and improved living conditions.

The crew remembers his professional methods and compares Lina's decisions with his.

His departure was not framed as betrayal.

People in this profession change jobs.

This is important to professional culture.

## 17. Crew Culture

### Competence First

**TEXT CANON**

The crew expects competence without requiring theatrics.

People test one another through work, questions, observation, and professional disagreement.

Captain Sen explicitly behaves as someone who has paid for competence and expects it without praise.

### Negotiated Belonging

**TEXT CANON**

The crew does not operate as a sentimental found-family declaration.

Lina begins as contract crew.

Mara gives her access to the room used for contract personnel.

Dara summarizes the group's internal logic simply:

they negotiate everything.

Belonging emerges through continued life and work.

### Existing Life Beyond the Newcomer

**TEXT CANON**

The crew's conversation does not automatically organize itself around Lina.

They:

* argue about old jobs;
* discuss people she does not know;
* share jokes without explaining them;
* exchange food;
* manage one another's small habits;
* continue existing as a group around her.

This is explicit in the current text.

The RPG must preserve this quality for established groups.

## 18. Existing Relationships

### Jax and Lio

**CURRENT CANON**

Jax and Lio are a romantic pair.

Their relationship should appear primarily through ordinary established behavior rather than constant romantic performance.

Current prose already shows habitual intimacy—for example Jax taking food from Lio while Lio deliberately positions preferred pieces within her reach.

### Mara

**CURRENT CANON**

Mara has a partner who works aboard a sky barge.

This relationship gives her life and obligations outside the immediate cutter crew.

Details remain **OPEN**.

### Rin / Dara / Others

**CURRENT CANON**

Do not automatically pair everyone romantically.

The crew contains different forms of intimacy, friendship, professional reliance, and private life.

## 19. Crew Stability

**CURRENT CANON / TEXT-SUPPORTED**

Sky work commonly involves temporary formations and changing personnel.

People may move because of:

* better work;
* weather;
* relationships;
* debt;
* restlessness;
* improved living conditions;
* professional opportunity.

Current prose directly frames this as normal within contract work.

The main crew is becoming more established, but permanence should not be assumed.

## 20. Professional Employment

### Contract Work

**TEXT CANON / CURRENT CANON**

Contract labor is normal.

Crews may be hired to protect or assist specific vessels.

Specialists may temporarily join established crews.

The relationship between employer, client, crew, captain, and specialist can vary.

### Jobs Take Time

**CURRENT CANON**

Most meaningful sky contracts involve:

* preparation;
* travel;
* execution;
* aftermath.

Many take at least a day when preparation and travel are counted.

Some are shorter.

Some take multiple days.

Do not treat sky work as instant missions selected from a board and completed before dinner unless the geography genuinely allows it.

## 21. Brokers and Guilds

### General Structure

**CURRENT CANON**

Professional work is supported by a network of:

* brokers;
* guild-like institutions;
* direct clients;
* repeat customers;
* professional referrals;
* crew reputation.

Exact institutional names remain **OPEN**.

The RPG may invent local brokers and offices.

Do not invent a single universal guild controlling all sky labor.

## 22. Contracts and Payment

### Common Structure

**CURRENT CANON**

A common professional arrangement may include:

* approximately half payment upfront;
* remaining payment after verified arrival or completion;
* bonuses;
* penalties;
* special risk terms.

This is a norm, not a universal law.

### Verification

**CURRENT CANON**

Completion can be verified through professional or institutional channels.

Because enforcement becomes harder across islands, reputation and reliable records matter.

### Vera Is Paid

**CURRENT CANON**

Vera is a professional provider.

Clients pay her for work.

Do not invert this relationship by having her routinely pay for the privilege of carrying jobs.

## 23. Reputation

### Professional Memory

**CURRENT CANON**

The sky-working community remembers performance.

Reputations may form around:

* reliability;
* price;
* risk tolerance;
* casualty rates;
* cargo survival;
* abandoning clients;
* saving damaged jobs;
* particular route expertise.

Reputation is contextual.

Different clients may value different things.

### Market Competition

**CURRENT CANON**

Many crews compete.

Possible market positions include:

* low-cost safe crews;
* high-risk loss-tolerant crews;
* corridor specialists;
* dominant local teams;
* uncharted-route specialists;
* barge-employed internal teams.

The player's crew is never assumed to be the only qualified option.

## 24. Barges and Internal Crews

**CURRENT CANON**

Some large barges employ their own protection or specialist teams.

Independent crews remain useful because not every vessel can justify permanent specialized staffing.

This creates competition between:

* independent contractors;
* retained specialists;
* internal crews;
* specialized solo operators.

## 25. Communications

### General

**CURRENT CANON**

The Sky Islands possess stable long-distance communications adequate for:

* business;
* contracts;
* family contact;
* scheduling;
* warnings;
* professional coordination.

The system is not globally instantaneous.

### Relay System

**CURRENT CANON**

Communication relies upon a relay network.

Exact technology and magical principles remain **OPEN**.

Information therefore possesses travel characteristics.

A message may be:

* delayed;
* routed;
* locally available before it reaches another island;
* known by professional networks before the general public.

### Social Consequence

**CURRENT CANON**

Different islands can possess different versions of the same developing event without communications being absurdly primitive.

This is intentional.

## 26. Ten-Year Migration Cycle

### Core Rule

**CURRENT CANON**

The Sky Islands undergo a major migration cycle approximately every ten years.

Islands transition between storm bands or otherwise traverse dangerous sections of the larger storm system.

The exact physical mechanism remains partially open.

The existence and approximately ten-year scale are current canon.

## 27. Migration Is Normal History, Not Apocalypse

**CURRENT CANON**

People have done this before.

Migration is dangerous and logistically enormous, but it is not treated as an unprecedented end-of-the-world event.

Institutions prepare for it.

Professionals specialize around it.

Infrastructure and routes exist partly because migration recurs.

The story should therefore derive pressure from:

* scale;
* limited resources;
* ecology;
* timing;
* competition;
* politics;
* unusual conditions;
* human decisions;

rather than everyone forgetting how migration works.

## 28. Migration Preparation

**CURRENT CANON**

Preparation begins well before actual major movement.

The RPG should allow migration to exist initially as background pressure.

Possible early signs include:

* contract shifts;
* infrastructure work;
* route surveys;
* increased purchasing;
* crew recruitment;
* barge preparation;
* scheduling disputes;
* changing insurance or rates;
* movement of livestock or equipment.

Do not immediately make migration the player's main plot.

## 29. Obfuscators During Migration

**CURRENT CANON — CORRECTED VERSION**

Obfuscators become more important, not less important, during major migration.

Earlier thinking suggested ordinary obfuscation might become unusable during migration.

That version is **SUPERSEDED**.

During migration:

* wildlife becomes more unsettled;
* mass traffic increases;
* cross-island movements need protection;
* large barges may require additional obfuscator coverage;
* preserved routes become strategically important.

## 30. Route Restrictions During Migration

**CURRENT CANON**

Many normal routes may close or become inappropriate.

Travel concentrates through routes that are:

* preserved;
* surveyed;
* maintained;
* patrolled;
* professionally supported.

This increases demand for skilled crews.

## 31. Wildlife During Migration

**CURRENT CANON**

Wildlife becomes more restless or behaviorally unstable as island movement approaches or occurs.

Exact biological causes remain **OPEN**.

This should not mean every animal becomes mindlessly aggressive.

Changed migration, breeding, feeding, territory, pressure, and magical conditions may all contribute.

## 32. Racing and Migration

**CURRENT CANON**

Organized racing circuits exist somewhere within Sky Islands culture.

They are not the core of Vera's professional identity.

Major circuits pause during migration year.

Do not turn the RPG into a racing-centered world unless the player deliberately enters that subculture.

## 33. Solo Runners

### General

**CURRENT CANON**

Solo runners occupy a precision niche.

A single runner generates less logistical and perceptual presence than a large crew or vessel formation.

They are useful for jobs where:

* speed;
* discretion;
* flexibility;
* small signature;
* responsiveness;

matter.

They are not inherently superior to normal transport.

## 34. Runner Limitations

**CURRENT CANON**

A runner has:

* limited cargo;
* less redundancy;
* personal exposure to danger;
* fewer people available when something fails.

They are specialized rather than universally optimal.

## 35. Retirement and Career Progression

**CURRENT CANON**

Not everyone dies in the sky.

Many professionals eventually leave high-risk work voluntarily.

Older or more cautious professionals may shift into:

* barge work;
* stable routes;
* training;
* liaison work;
* planning;
* brokerage;
* other support positions.

Sky work should therefore feel like a profession with career paths, not a death cult.

## 36. Bad Jobs

**CURRENT CANON**

A job can be judged bad for reasons beyond death.

Indicators include:

* unexpected equipment loss;
* loss of bonded beast or craft;
* significant injury;
* lingering effects;
* negative profit;
* damaged professional reputation;
* damaged client relationship;
* failure to meet contractual expectations.

A job where everyone survives can still be a disastrous contract.

## 37. Preparation and After-Action Work

**CURRENT CANON**

Professional crews prepare for specific corridors and jobs.

They also produce documentation afterward.

Possible requirements include:

* incident reporting;
* official reports;
* internal crew records;
* equipment assessment;
* client verification.

The exact bureaucratic framework remains **OPEN**.

## 53. Genre and Setting Boundaries

**CURRENT CANON**

The Sky Islands should support:

* adventure;
* professional life;
* travel;
* exploration;
* slice-of-life;
* interpersonal relationships;
* economic pressure;
* ecological danger;
* long-term migration;
* occasional larger political or regional movement.

The region is not inherently:

* post-apocalyptic;
* grimdark;
* a fantasy western;
* a racing story;
* a chosen-one setting.

## 54. Fantasy vs. Science-Fiction Texture

**CURRENT CANON**

The setting should read primarily as fantasy / science-fantasy rather than disguised modern machinery with fantasy nouns.

Technology and magic coexist in practical systems.

Characters may use specialized tools with repeatable behavior.

The narrative does not need to provide engineering-level explanations for every magical mechanism.

## 55. No Identity-as-Key Worldbuilding

**REJECTED DESIGN DIRECTION**

Do not build major Sky Islands conflicts around:

* someone being important because of what they inherently are;
* unique bloodline access;
* identity as magical currency;
* identity as a mythic key;
* the world recognizing the protagonist as inherently special.

Importance should come primarily through choices, competence, relationships, circumstance, and accumulated action.

## 56. No Universal Plot Conspiracy

**CURRENT DESIGN CONSTRAINT**

Do not make:

* every route problem;
* every guild;
* every wildlife anomaly;
* every migration issue;
* every character history;

part of a single secret scheme.

Independent systems and unrelated problems must exist.

## 57. Do Not Make the Sky Empty

**TEXT CANON / CURRENT CANON**

Open air is not simply blank space.

Even when a vessel sees no nearby ship, the environment may contain:

* cloud;
* pressure systems;
* island wake;
* lightning;
* rain;
* magical discharge;
* wildlife;
* distant traffic signatures.

This is especially important because obfuscation in open sky is difficult precisely because there is not always conventional visual clutter to hide behind.

The crew manipulates patterns and attention, not just visual cover.

## 62. Repository Rule for Existing Novel Characters

The current novel cast may exist in RPG continuity.

They are not automatically:

* party members;
* quest givers;
* romantic options;
* mentors;
* central to the player's life.

A player may hear about them before meeting them.

## 63. Repository Rule for Novel Events

Current Chapter One establishes setting reality and some character facts.

The RPG does not need to reenact Chapter One.

Unless the campaign explicitly uses novel continuity at the same moment, treat the novel as a source of setting canon rather than a mandatory plot.

## 64. Default World Assumption

When no special crisis is occurring:

* ships are traveling;
* markets are operating;
* crews are competing for work;
* repairs are being made;
* people are changing jobs;
* barges are carrying goods;
* routes are being monitored;
* wildlife is behaving according to its own ecology;
* storms are changing;
* communications are moving;
* families are living ordinary lives;
* migration preparations exist somewhere in the larger cycle.

The world functions.

## 65. The Sky Islands' Central Experiential Rule

The skies are dangerous enough to create professions.

They are not so dangerous that civilization makes no sense.

The storm should produce:

* work;
* expertise;
* culture;
* caution;
* opportunity;
* beauty;
* inconvenience;
* loss;
* attachment.

People do not spend every waking moment being afraid of it.

For many of them, it is simply where life happens.

## 66. Repository Summary for the RPG Engine

When generating Sky Islands play, assume:

Floating inhabited islands exist amid layered magical storm systems.

Sky travel is normal and professionally organized.

Routes, forecasts, equipment, and competence matter.

Storm wildlife is intelligent enough in behavior to require interpretation, but should not automatically be anthropomorphized.

Obfuscators manage attention and signatures rather than casting invisibility.

Large barges and smaller specialist craft fill different economic niches.

Solo runners such as Vera provide specialized precision service.

Communication is stable but not instantaneous.

Contract work and reputation structure much of professional sky life.

The approximately ten-year island migration is recurring, dangerous, and civilization-scale, but not apocalyptic.

The world contains competent people who existed before the player.

Current prose is authoritative where it establishes concrete setting function.

Legacy Talkar lore is not automatically Sky Islands canon.

Open questions remain open until deliberately resolved.
